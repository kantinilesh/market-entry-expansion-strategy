## 1. Why This Phase Exists

Phase 06 produced a single-point estimate of break-even and ROI for each candidate market. Phase 07 answers the essential follow-up question that every Board of Directors will ask:

> *"What happens to your recommendation if your assumptions are wrong?"*

A single forecast is dangerous because it:
1. **Creates false precision** — presenting one number implies certainty that does not exist.
2. **Hides risk asymmetry** — downside losses often exceed upside gains (costs can spike faster than prices can rise).
3. **Anchors decision-makers** — once executives see "Month 14 break-even," they anchor on that specific number and fail to prepare for alternative outcomes.

Scenario analysis replaces a single forecast with a **range of plausible futures**, enabling management to make informed capital allocation decisions.

## 2. Concepts Explained

### 2.1 Base Case

The **base case** is the most-likely scenario, built on reasonable assumptions grounded in verified data. It is not optimistic or pessimistic — it represents the consulting team's best estimate of what will happen if market conditions follow their expected trajectory.

**Simple Example**: A coffee shop projecting next year's revenue might use the base case of 200 cups/day at $4.50 per cup, based on last year's average. It assumes nothing dramatically changes.

**NovaHome Application**: Our base case uses Phase 06's verified contribution margins ($213.68 Netherlands, $177.67 UK, $191.00 Australia, $192.12 Germany) and the established volume ramp profiles.

### 2.2 Upside Case

The **upside case** models a plausible optimistic outcome — not a fantasy, but a scenario where multiple favorable conditions materialize simultaneously.

**Simple Example**: The coffee shop assumes a new office building opens nearby (+20% foot traffic), it wins a local media feature (+6% pricing power), and a competitor closes (−15% marketing spend needed).

**NovaHome Application**: Demand exceeds forecast by 20%, NovaHome successfully positions as a premium brand (+6% ASP), organic referrals reduce CAC by 15%, and favorable FX translation adds 5%.

### 2.3 Downside Case

The **downside case** models a plausible pessimistic outcome — not a catastrophe, but a scenario where multiple headwinds materialize simultaneously.

**Simple Example**: The coffee shop faces a recession (−25% foot traffic), a price-war from a new competitor (−8% pricing), and rising supply costs (+10% beans, +20% rent).

**NovaHome Application**: Demand is 25% below forecast, incumbent price competition forces −8% ASP reductions, digital ad market inflation pushes CAC up 25%, and supply chain disruptions inflate shipping by 20%.

### 2.4 Sensitivity Analysis

**Sensitivity analysis** varies one input at a time while holding all others constant, revealing which individual assumptions most influence the output.

**Simple Example**: If raising coffee price by 10% improves annual profit by $15,000, but reducing rent by 10% only saves $3,000, then the business is more **sensitive** to pricing than to rent.

**NovaHome Application**: Our tornado analysis (Sheet 2) reveals that UK break-even is most sensitive to **Sales Volume** and **Selling Price**, and least sensitive to **Launch Capex**.

### 2.5 Risk vs. Uncertainty

- **Risk** = events with known probabilities (e.g., "There is a 30% chance container freight rates increase by 20%").
- **Uncertainty** = events with unknown or unknowable probabilities (e.g., "A new pandemic could shut down Vietnamese factories").

Scenarios handle **risk**. For true **uncertainty**, we define **decision triggers** (Section 6) — observable signals that should cause management to pause and reassess.

### 2.6 Scenario Planning

Scenario planning is the discipline of constructing multiple coherent, internally consistent future states and testing strategies against each. Unlike sensitivity analysis (which varies one input), scenarios vary **multiple correlated inputs simultaneously** — because in reality, recessions don't just reduce demand; they also increase competitive intensity, depress pricing, and inflate customer acquisition costs concurrently.

### 2.7 Why One Forecast Is Dangerous

In the 2008 financial crisis, most banks relied on single-point models that assumed housing prices would continue rising. When prices fell, the models produced no guidance because they had never been tested against adverse conditions.

**For NovaHome**: If the Board approves expansion based solely on "Month 14 break-even" and actual demand comes in 25% below forecast, there is no pre-agreed contingency plan. The company bleeds cash without a decision framework for when to cut losses.

## 3. Scenario Assumption Framework

All scenario multipliers are applied to the Phase 06 verified base-case inputs. Adjustments are **asymmetric** — costs can spike faster than they can be reduced — reflecting real-world market dynamics.

| Variable | Downside | Base | Upside | Rationale |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Sales Volume** | −25% | 0% | +20% | Demand elasticity under economic slowdown vs. brand momentum |
| **Selling Price (ASP)** | −8% | 0% | +6% | Pricing pressure from incumbents vs. premium positioning |
| **Customer Acquisition Cost** | +25% | 0% | −15% | Ad market inflation / CPM spikes vs. organic referral growth |
| **Product Cost (FOB)** | +10% | 0% | −5% | Supply chain disruption vs. volume procurement discount |
| **Shipping / Freight** | +20% | 0% | −10% | Container rate volatility vs. consolidated routing efficiency |
| **Launch Capex** | +15% | 0% | −5% | Regulatory cost overrun vs. streamlined certification |
| **Monthly Fixed Opex** | +20% | 0% | −10% | Cost inflation + FX headwinds vs. lean operations |
| **Currency Impact (FX)** | −10% | 0% | +5% | USD strengthening erodes revenue vs. favorable FX |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Asymmetry is deliberate.** In practice, costs escalate faster than they decline (e.g., CAC can spike +25% in a competitive ad auction, but rarely drops −25%). Revenue improvements are harder to achieve (+6% ASP requires active brand investment) than revenue losses (−8% ASP can happen passively from competitive pressure).

## 4. Results: Per-Market Scenario Analysis

### 4.1 United Kingdom

| Metric | Upside | Base | Downside |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Adjusted ASP | $1,013 | $910 | $745 |
| Contribution / Unit | $361.47 | $177.67 | −$125.29 |
| Contribution Margin | 35.7% | 19.5% | −16.6% |
| Break-Even Month | **Month 11** | **Month 22** | **Not Achieved** |
| 3-Year ROI | 1,163.0% | 250.8% | −596.2% |

**Assessment**: The UK's high volume ramp (40 → 550 units/month over 24 months) enables strong profitability under base and upside conditions. Under base case, 24-month cash flow projections show break-even extending to Month 22 — beyond the Board's 18-month hurdle but still achieving positive cash flow within the 36-month evaluation window. Under downside, unit economics turn negative, making expansion unviable.

**Classification**: **CONDITIONALLY VIABLE** — Passes the 36-month evaluation window under base case but exceeds the 18-month hurdle ceiling. Requires active demand monitoring and cost discipline.

| Contribution / Unit | $370.16 | $192.12 | −$101.16 |

**Assessment**: Germany's higher launch capex ($680,000 including $250,000 app localization) creates a steeper upfront cash deficit. **Classification**: **CONDITIONALLY VIABLE** — Profitable over 36 months under base case but requires patience. The $250,000 app localization capex is the primary driver of extended payback.

### 4.3 Netherlands

| Metric | Upside | Base | Downside |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Adjusted ASP | $1,046 | $940 | $770 |
| Contribution / Unit | $382.51 | $213.68 | −$63.71 |
| Contribution Margin | 36.6% | 22.7% | −8.2% |
| Break-Even Month | **Not Achieved (24 mo)** | **Not Achieved (24 mo)** | **Not Achieved** |
| 3-Year ROI | 147.3% | −98.3% | −329.0% |

**Assessment**: Despite having the **highest per-unit contribution margin** ($213.68, 22.7%) and lowest capex ($390,000), the Netherlands suffers from **insufficient volume scale**. The monthly ramp peaks at 138 units/month — roughly 25% of the UK's terminal volume. The fixed cost base ($20,000/month × 24 = $480,000) plus launch capex ($390,000) requires a cumulative volume and contribution that the Netherlands' addressable customer base struggles to generate within 24 months.

This finding modifies our Phase 06 assessment. The Phase 06 simplified payback formula (which used a Year 2 average of 250 units/month across all markets) produced a Month 12 break-even. However, the **realistic market-specific volume ramp** for a country of 18 million people cannot sustain 250 units/month.

> [!WARNING]
> **Reassessment Required**: The Netherlands' Phase 06 break-even of Month 12 was based on a generalized volume assumption (250 units/mo). Under a realistic Netherlands-specific ramp profile (peaking at ~138 units/mo), break-even extends significantly. This recommendation is sensitive to the achievable monthly volume and requires further validation of demand estimates.

**Classification**: **FRAGILE** — Excellent unit economics undermined by market scale constraints. Viable only as a **secondary market** entered after the UK infrastructure (3PL, brand awareness) is established, enabling shared fixed costs.

**Classification**: **FRAGILE** — Requires upside conditions to meet the 18-month hurdle. Under base case, the market is approximately break-even at the 36-month mark. Recommended as a **Phase 2 market** (2027+) after European operations provide cash flow stability.

## 5. Sensitivity Analysis: What Matters Most

### 5.1 Tornado Analysis Results (United Kingdom)

The tornado analysis isolates the impact of each variable individually on UK break-even timing:

| Impact Rank | Variable | Favorable (BE Month) | Base (BE Month) | Adverse (BE Month) | Spread |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **#1** | Sales Volume | Month 16 | Month 22 | Not Achieved | **8+** |
| **#2** | Selling Price (ASP) | Month 16 | Month 22 | Not Achieved | **8+** |
| **#3** | Currency Impact (FX) | Month 17 | Month 22 | Not Achieved | **7+** |
| **#4** | Customer Acquisition Cost | Month 18 | Month 22 | Not Achieved | **6+** |
| **#5** | Shipping Cost | Month 19 | Month 22 | Not Achieved | **5+** |
| **#6** | Product Cost (FOB) | Month 20 | Month 22 | Not Achieved | **4+** |
| **#7** | Monthly Fixed Opex | Month 20 | Month 22 | Not Achieved | **4+** |
| **#8** | Launch Capex | Month 20 | Month 22 | Not Achieved | **3+** |

### 5.2 Key Takeaways

1. **Sales volume is the #1 driver.** The single most impactful variable is whether NovaHome achieves its demand ramp. A 30% volume shortfall can push break-even beyond 24 months; a 30% volume overachievement brings it within 16 months.

2. **Selling price is the #2 driver.** Price elasticity is critical. NovaHome's positioning in the "affordable premium" whitespace ($600–$1,200) protects against luxury price wars, but any broad discounting trend would compress margins severely.

3. **FX is the hidden risk.** Currency impact ranks #3 because NovaHome manufactures in USD-denominated Vietnam/Taiwan but sells in GBP/EUR/AUD. A 15% USD strengthening would erode all revenue and contribution margins simultaneously.

4. **Launch capex is the least sensitive variable.** Even a 30% capex overrun only shifts break-even by ~2–3 months. This is because capex is a one-time cost; ongoing unit economics and volume dominate the cash flow trajectory.

## 7. Management Monitoring Checklist & Decision Triggers

### 7.1 What Management Should Monitor Monthly

| KPI | Threshold | Action If Breached |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Monthly unit sales vs. ramp forecast | Actual < 80% of forecast for 3 consecutive months | Trigger cost reduction review |
| Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) | CAC > $275 (UK) for 2 consecutive months | Shift marketing mix to partnerships/organic channels |
| Average Selling Price (ASP) realized | ASP < $850 (UK) for 2 consecutive months | Review competitive pricing landscape |
| Container freight rate (40ft HC) | Rate > $4,500/container (20%+ above baseline) | Negotiate long-term freight contracts or delay inventory restocking |
| GBP/USD exchange rate | GBP weakens below $1.15 | Evaluate GBP-denominated price increase |
| Monthly cumulative cash deficit | Deficit exceeds $600,000 at Month 8 | Escalate to Board for additional capital or pause assessment |

### 7.2 What Would Invalidate the Recommendation

The following conditions, if observed, would require **a fundamental reassessment** of the expansion thesis:

1. **Volume collapse**: Realized monthly demand is <50% of forecast for 6 consecutive months, indicating a structural demand miscalculation rather than a temporary slowdown.
2. **Price war initiation**: Peloton or a well-funded competitor launches a mid-market product at <$700, directly targeting NovaHome's whitespace.
3. **Supply chain disruption**: Factory shutdown or geopolitical event disrupting Vietnam/Taiwan manufacturing for >90 days.
4. **Regulatory surprise**: Post-launch discovery of a mandatory product redesign requirement (e.g., battery safety directive) with >$200,000 compliance cost.
5. **Currency shock**: GBP/USD falls below $1.05 or EUR/USD falls below $0.90, eroding all contribution margin.

## 8. Honest Limitations of This Analysis

1. **Volume ramp profiles are assumed, not validated.** The 24-month demand ramps are synthetic estimates based on market size ratios. Real-world adoption curves depend on brand awareness, marketing effectiveness, and competitive response — none of which can be precisely modeled ex ante.

2. **Scenarios assume independence.** Our scenarios combine multiple adverse conditions, but we cannot model every possible correlation structure. For example, a global recession could simultaneously reduce demand, strengthen the USD, and increase competitive intensity in ways our scenarios may underestimate.

3. **No probabilistic weighting.** We do not assign probabilities to Base, Upside, or Downside scenarios because the inputs lack the statistical foundation for reliable probability estimation. The scenarios should be understood as **plausible narratives**, not probabilistic forecasts.

4. **Terminal rate extrapolation.** The 3-year ROI extrapolates Month 24's terminal volume forward for 12 additional months. Actual Year 3 performance depends on subscription retention, repeat hardware purchases, and competitive dynamics that this model does not capture.

5. **Hardware-only economics.** This analysis conservatively evaluates hardware contribution margin alone. The $19.99/month NovaConnected subscription generates high-margin recurring revenue (76.5% gross margin) that would significantly improve the payback picture — but was excluded to maintain conservative rigor.
