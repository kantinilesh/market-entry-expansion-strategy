## 1. Executive Purpose & Financial Governance

Market size alone (Phase 04) and competitive whitespace (Phase 05) are insufficient to authorize capital investment. Phase 06 evaluates the **micro-level commercial engine**:
> *"Does NovaHome generate cash profit on every single physical unit delivered in a target country, and how many months does it take for cumulative profits to repay upfront market entry investments?"*

To maintain data integrity and avoid forced optimistic conclusions:
* Every formula is strictly governed by GAAP/consulting standards.
* Negative contribution margins and unachieved paybacks are reported transparently.
* No numbers are forced: if a market cannot break even within 18 months, it is explicitly flagged.

## 2. Core Financial Formulas & Definitions

### 1. Revenue ($R$)
The total gross inflow generated from customer purchases:
$$\text{Revenue} = \text{Units Sold} \times \text{Average Selling Price (ASP)}$$

### 2. Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
The direct inventory and factory manufacturing costs incurred to produce the physical equipment:
$$\text{COGS} = \text{Units Sold} \times \text{FOB Factory Manufacturing Cost}$$
* *NovaHome Baseline*: $450.00 USD per blended hardware unit (manufactured in Vietnam/Taiwan).

### 3. Gross Profit & Gross Margin ($GM$)
The financial spread remaining after deducting factory product costs from revenue:
$$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Revenue} - \text{COGS}$$
$$\text{Gross Margin } (\%) = \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 100$$

### 4. Fixed Costs vs. Variable Costs
* **Variable Costs ($VC$)**: Costs that scale directly and linearly with each unit sold. If NovaHome sells 0 units, variable costs are $0.
  $$\text{Variable Cost per Unit} = \text{FOB Product Cost} + \text{Inbound Ocean Freight} + \text{Import Tariffs} + \text{Last-Mile 3PL Delivery} + \text{Payment Fee} + \text{Warranty Reserve} + \text{CAC}$$
* **Fixed Costs ($FC$)**: Operating overhead that must be paid regardless of unit sales volume:
  * *Upfront Launch Capex*: Regulatory safety certifications (CE/UKCA), app localization, initial brand launch campaign.
  * *Ongoing Monthly Fixed Opex*: 3PL minimum pallet storage retainer, localized customer service agency retainer, local legal/tax administration.

### 5. Delivered Contribution Margin ($M_c$)
The cash profit generated by each unit sold that "contributes" toward paying down fixed overhead:
$$\text{Contribution per Unit } (C_u) = \text{ASP} - \text{Total Variable Cost per Unit}$$
$$\text{Contribution Margin } (M_c \%) = \frac{C_u}{\text{ASP}} \times 100$$
* *Critical Consulting Rule*: If $C_u \le 0$, the business model is insolvent—every additional unit sold burns cash.

### 6. Break-Even Units ($Q_{\text{BE}}$)
The exact volume of physical units that must be sold to cover total fixed costs:
$$Q_{\text{BE}} = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Costs}}{C_u}$$
* *Safety Rule*: If $C_u \le 0$, Break-Even is mathematically undefined and reported as **"Not Achieved / Insolvent"**.

### 7. Break-Even Revenue ($R_{\text{BE}}$)
The dollar sales required to break even:
$$R_{\text{BE}} = Q_{\text{BE}} \times \text{ASP} = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Costs}}{M_c}$$

### 8. Cash-Flow Payback Period ($T_{\text{payback}}$)
The exact calendar month where cumulative net cash flow equals or exceeds the initial upfront capital outlay:
$$\text{Cumulative Cash Flow}_t = -I_0 + \sum_{m=1}^{t} (\text{Monthly Contribution}_m - \text{Monthly Fixed Opex}_m)$$
$$T_{\text{payback}} = \text{First month } t \text{ where } \text{Cumulative Cash Flow}_t \ge 0$$
* *NovaHome Hurdle*: Must achieve break-even within **$\le 18\text{ months}$** from launch.

### 9. Return on Investment (ROI)
The net financial gain generated over a 36-month investment horizon relative to initial capital deployed:
$$\text{ROI } (\%) = \frac{\text{Cumulative Net Profit over 36 Months}}{\text{Initial Upfront Investment}} \times 100$$

## 4. Why Accounting Profit and Cash Flow are Fundamentally Different

A frequent error made by novice analysts is confusing **Accrual Accounting Net Income** with **Cash Flow**:

1. **Working Capital Inventory Burden**: 
   * Under accrual accounting, purchasing $1,000,000 of inventory is not an expense—it is an asset on the balance sheet.
   * In reality, NovaHome must pay the factory **cash upfront** 60–90 days before the container arrives at the port. This creates a severe **cash trough** that can bankrupt a growing company even if paper accounting shows positive gross margins!
2. **Upfront Capex Amortization**:
   * Accounting spreads a $250k software localization fee over 36 months ($6,944/month depreciation).
   * Cash flow requires wiring the entire $250,000 check on Day 1.
3. **Cash Break-Even Governance**:
   * NovaHome’s Board evaluates **Monthly Cash Flow Break-Even**, which tracks the physical cash balance in the bank account, ensuring expansion does not deplete domestic liquidity.

## 5. Model Limitations & Stress Factors

1. **Foreign Exchange (FX) Volatility**: Re-pricing hardware dynamically when local currencies swing by $>10\%$ vs. the USD.
2. **Channel Mix Assumptions**: The baseline models assume a **Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) 3PL fulfillment model**. If NovaHome pivots to retail wholesale (e.g., Decathlon or John Lewis), gross selling price is discounted by 35%–40%, but CAC and last-mile logistics shift to the retail partner.
3. **Hardware-Only vs. Subscription LTV**: This unit economics model conservatively evaluates **Hardware Contribution Margin alone**. In practice, every active buyer generates an additional high-margin software annuity ($19.99/mo @ 76.5% gross margin), providing significant upside margin cushion.
